The Proportion Guide

Proportion is the quiet reason some outfits look effortless. It is about lengths and volumes, and it is easy to adjust once you know what to look for.

Lengths: the rule of thirds

Your outfit divides your body into sections. Uneven splits, roughly one-third to two-thirds, look more natural than an even half-and-half. A hip-length top over trousers often cuts you in half; a front tuck or a cropped top fixes it instantly.

Volume: one relaxed piece at a time

If your top is…Try a bottom that is…
OversizedSlim or straight, or a pencil skirt
FittedWide-leg, pleated or A-line
CroppedHigh-waisted, any width
RegularStraight, with a half-tuck to define the waist

Volume on volume can work, but it needs a defined waist or a strong shoe to anchor it.

Where your waist sits

A high waist lengthens the leg; a low waist lengthens the body. Neither is better. Choose based on which proportion you want to emphasise and how comfortable you are when sitting.

Hems and shoes

  • Wide trousers: hem should nearly touch the floor in the shoes you wear most.
  • Straight trousers: a slight break or ankle length.
  • Midi skirts: pair with a shoe that shows a little foot, or a boot that meets the hem.
  • Cropped trousers: show the ankle; a low shoe keeps the line clean.

Tucks and rolls

A front tuck, a rolled sleeve or a cuffed hem changes proportion without changing clothes. These small adjustments are the quickest way to make an outfit look considered.

Test it in a mirror

Step back from a full-length mirror or take a photo. If the eye stops in the middle of your body, adjust the length; if everything looks the same width, add or remove volume in one place. The Proportion Balancer suggests a quick fix.
